The curriculum

What your child will actually learn

Three seasons, seventy-two lessons, built on the Kodály sequence used by primary music teachers worldwide. Follow it in order and a five-year-old finishes able to read rhythm, sing in tune from hand signs, and write four bars of their own.

Why Kodály

Zoltán Kodály’s method starts with the two instruments every child already owns — the voice and the body. Singing and clapping come before notation, and notation arrives only once the child can already hear what it represents.

It’s the reason our first lessons contain no sheet music at all. A child who can clap a rhythm accurately will read it in minutes. A child who reads it first usually learns to decode symbols without ever hearing them.

A note on solfège for French, Italian and Arabic speakers

We teach relative solfège — we sing the distances between notes, so the same pattern works in any key. If your child learns fixed do at school, where Do always means C, both systems fit together comfortably. We simply never say “Do is home.”

About

Made by a composer, for small people

Tiny Maestros started with a simple frustration: there is an enormous amount of children’s music online, and almost none of it teaches children anything about music.

Why this exists

I trained in film scoring. I spent years learning how music carries feeling, how a phrase resolves, why a particular interval makes a room go quiet. Then I started looking for something to share that with a child, and found shelf after shelf of songs about counting to ten.

There is nothing wrong with those songs. But somewhere between the nursery rhymes for toddlers and the theory channels made for teenagers, an entire age group gets skipped — precisely the years when a child’s ear is most open, and when learning to hear music properly is easiest.

So I started writing the lessons I couldn’t find. Every melody in Tiny Maestros is original and composed for the concept it teaches. Nothing is a rearranged nursery rhyme, and nothing is generated. When a song teaches steady beat, it is built so that a child cannot help but feel the beat while singing it.

How the lessons are built

The curriculum follows the Kodály sequence, which primary music teachers use around the world. It begins with the two instruments every child already owns — voice and body — and only introduces written notation once the child can already hear what the symbols mean.

Each lesson has exactly one objective and runs about three minutes, which is roughly how long a five-year-old will genuinely attend. The characters are there to carry the idea, not to decorate it: Rest is a small creature who never makes a sound, because a rest is the hardest thing to explain to a child and much the easiest to introduce.

A note on how these are made

The music is composed, performed and mixed by a human. The animation is produced with the help of modern digital tools, in the way most small independent studios now work, with every character designed and every scene directed by hand. We think you should know how the things your children watch are made.

Who this is for

Children between five and ten, and the grown-ups sitting with them. You do not need to read music yourself — a great many of our families are learning alongside their children, which is honestly the best way for both of you.

Teachers are welcome too. The sequence is designed to sit alongside a primary music scheme rather than replace it, and the Classroom plan includes a printing licence.

What we will never do

  • Collect any information about a child. Accounts belong to adults, and we don’t ask children for anything.
  • Put advertising in front of children on our own site.
  • Move free lessons behind the paywall after the fact.
  • Use manufactured urgency, fake scarcity or countdown timers to sell you something.

The whole policy in one sentence

We do not collect any personal information from children, because we have built this site so that children never have anything to give us.

How we designed around this

Children’s privacy law — the US Children’s Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA), the UK’s Children’s Code and the EU’s rules on minors — puts serious obligations on any service that collects information from children. The simplest and safest way to meet those obligations is not to collect anything at all, so that is what we do.

In practice:

  • There are no children’s accounts. A membership belongs to an adult. We never ask for a child’s name, age, birthday, photograph, school or location.
  • Nothing on this site asks a child for anything. No quizzes that collect answers, no “tell us your name” forms, no leaderboards, no profiles.
  • No advertising, ever, on our own site. No ad networks, no behavioural tracking, no retargeting pixels.
  • No chat, comments or uploads. Children cannot post anything here, so there is nothing for us to store or moderate.
  • Our mailing list is for adults. The signup forms say so, and anyone under 18 is asked to have a grown-up sign up instead.

What about the videos?

Our lessons are published on YouTube and are marked “Made for Kids”, as the law requires for content aimed at children. That designation means YouTube switches off personalised advertising, comments and notifications on our videos, and limits the data collected from viewers.

What happens on YouTube is governed by Google’s privacy policy, not ours. We have no access to information about individual viewers — we can see how many people watched a video, and nothing about who they were.

If we embed a video on this site, we use privacy-enhanced embedding where it is available. Pressing play still connects you to YouTube, so their policy applies from that moment.

Payments

Payments are made by adults, on our payment provider’s own secure pages. No child is ever asked for payment information, and no card details reach us at any point.

If something has gone wrong

If you believe your child has somehow given us personal information — for example, if they used your device and submitted an email address — please write to . We will find it, delete it, and confirm to you that it has been deleted. You do not need to prove anything or fill in a form.

You can also ask us at any time to confirm what we hold about your household, or to delete all of it.

How long refunds take

We process refunds within two working days of your email. After that it’s up to your bank — usually 5 to 10 working days for the money to appear, occasionally a little longer. The refund goes back to the card or account you paid with; our payment provider won’t send it anywhere else.
